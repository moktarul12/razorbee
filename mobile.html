Apps people keep.

Mobile is not a smaller website. It is a promise you make in a pocket — offline, in another language, on a Tuesday commute.

The pocket is the product

We design for thumbs, interrupted attention, and flaky networks. A screen that only works on office Wi-Fi is a demo, not a product. Razorbee ships iOS and Android — native when the platform demands it, Flutter or React Native when one codebase should serve both stores without feeling borrowed.

Release is part of the design. TestFlight and internal tracks, review notes that a human at Apple or Google can follow, versioning that support teams can explain, and crash reporting that names the build, not the mood.

What a mobile engagement covers

Discovery that names the job the phone is hired to do. Information architecture for one-handed use. Offline queues and honest sync. Push that earns the interruption. Deep links from email, SMS, and QR. Analytics that respect privacy laws in the regions you sell. A backend that does not assume the client is always a browser.

We have shipped donation and logistics flows for ManavSathi, and mobile-ready journeys beside brokerage products like METRÕ & HäUS. The same discipline applies when the brief is a wallet, a field tool, or a patient companion.

Global, not a single store listing

Australia, Singapore, the Gulf, Europe, and the US do not share a review culture or a network. We plan store accounts, tax, age ratings, and localization before the first binary. Time zones are a feature: Bangalore builds, Sydney wakes up to a build that already passed CI.
